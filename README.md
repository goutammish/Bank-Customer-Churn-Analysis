# Bank Customer Churn Analysis

## Project Objective
The objective of this project is to analyze customer churn for a bank by visualizing and identifying patterns in customer demographics, activity, and product preferences. The analysis aims to provide actionable insights into the factors contributing to customer churn and help the bank design strategies to retain customers.

---

## Process
1. **Data Collection**: Bank customer data was gathered, including demographic details, activity status, product preferences, credit scores, and churn status.
2. **Data Cleaning and Preparation**: The data was cleaned and organized to ensure accurate analysis.
3. **Visualization in Power BI**: Multiple interactive visualizations were created in Power BI to explore relationships and trends among key metrics such as churn rate, customer demographics, and activity.

---

## Questions Addressed
1. What is the churn rate among the bank's customers?
2. How does churn vary by customer demographics (gender, age group, and country)?
3. What is the impact of credit card ownership and activity status on churn rates?
4. How do different products and credit score ranges influence churn?
5. Which customer segments show the highest churn rates?

---

## Project Insights
The analysis provides the following key insights:
1. **Gender**: The distribution of male and female customers is relatively balanced, with a similar churn trend observed.
2. **Activity Status**: Inactive customers show a significantly higher churn rate compared to active customers.
3. **Credit Card Ownership**: Customers without credit cards have a higher churn rate.
4. **Country Analysis**: Churn rates vary by country, with noticeable differences among customers in France, Germany, and Spain.
5. **Product Preferences**: Certain products (e.g., Product 3) are associated with higher churn compared to others.
6. **Age Group**: Customers in the 31-40 age group have the highest churn rate, while older customers tend to churn less frequently.
7. **Credit Scores**: Customers with mid-range credit scores (601-700) are more likely to churn, while those with extremely high or low credit scores exhibit lower churn rates.

---

## Conclusions
1. Customer churn is influenced by multiple factors, including activity status, product usage, and credit card ownership.
2. Certain customer segments, such as inactive customers, those aged 31-40, and customers without credit cards, require targeted retention strategies.
3. Focused campaigns can be designed to improve product satisfaction for customers associated with high-churn products.
4. Cross-selling credit cards and incentivizing activity among dormant accounts could reduce churn rates.

By leveraging these insights, the bank can adopt data-driven approaches to enhance customer retention and reduce churn.
